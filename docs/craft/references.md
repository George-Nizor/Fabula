# References: styles worth stealing from

Taste comes from having seen a lot of good work. These are widely watched styles of
talking-head and explanatory video, described as recipes — what they do with the picture,
the type, the rhythm — so a composition can borrow a *method* rather than imitate a look.
None of it is a brand to copy; the theme presets are Fabula's own. Use these when the person
says "make it feel like…" or when a film's purpose points at one of them.

## The explainer (Vox, Johnny Harris, Kurzgesagt lineage)

- The argument is built in the picture. A map, a chart, a diagram *develops* over a passage,
  each sentence adding one thing to it, rather than a finished card appearing.
- Source material is shown as source material: a scanned page, a photograph with its
  credit, archival footage labelled as such. The credibility is in the showing.
- Type is a narrator's aside: a short kicker in caps, a big line that names the point, a
  small credit. Never a paragraph.
- The head appears at the moments of judgment — "and this is the part that matters" — and
  otherwise gets out of the way. Long cutaways, purposeful returns.
- Fabula: `cutaway` with `flow`/`timeline`/`custom` that arrives in two or three scenes
  rather than one; `image` with attribution; `headline` and `section` for the turns;
  the `broadcast` or `slate` preset; `dissolve` transitions.

## The tech reviewer (MKBHD lineage)

- The product is the star; the head is a co-presenter. Clean, still shots of the thing,
  edge to edge, with a word or two of type.
- Numbers are shown large and briefly. One at a time.
- Restraint: a single accent colour, one display face, generous space. Nothing glows.
- The rhythm is even. Changes land on the sentence, not before it, and nothing moves for
  the sake of moving.
- Fabula: `image` with `pop` or `tilt` motion in `pip` or `full`; `stat` and `big-number`
  for the specs; `mono` or `ink` preset; `cut` or short `glide` transitions; no kinetic type.

## The educator (Ali Abdaal, 3Blue1Brown lineage)

- The structure is shown: "three things", numbered, returned to. The viewer always knows
  where they are in the lesson.
- Diagrams are built to be understood, not to be pretty, and they are on screen long enough
  to be read twice.
- The head is warm and present; the film returns to it often, usually on a question.
- Fabula: `teaser` after the opening, `section` with numbers, `steps` and `flow`, `question`
  at the turns, `list` where a list is genuinely being taught; `paper` or `dawn` preset.

## The creator-essay (the "video essay" lineage)

- Long passages of the head alone; the picture changes at paragraph boundaries, not sentence
  boundaries. Space is the point.
- Quotes are set as quotes: the words on a dark stage, a name, a source.
- Section titles are chapter headings in a book, with a number.
- Fabula: `focus` for most of the film; `quote`, `post`, `headline`, `section`; the serif
  face for titles; `glide` at its slowest; the `ink` or `studio` preset.

## The broadcast package (news and documentary)

- Lower thirds: a name, a role, a place, in a bar. Arrive and leave on a cut.
- The screen is a stage: split screens, a panel beside the head, a graphic that owns the
  frame for one fact.
- Sober type, high contrast, tabular numbers.
- Fabula: `block` title style, `bar` callouts, `chart` and `share`, `compare`; `broadcast`
  preset; `cut` transitions; `screen` graphics for footage.

## Short-form (the feed)

- Something on screen at frame one. Captions from the first word, large, one phrase at a
  time, the current word emphasised.
- Every three to five seconds something changes: a word slams in, a zoom lands, a card
  interrupts. Energy is the aesthetic; restraint reads as absence.
- One idea, spoken fast, ending on a line or an ask.
- Fabula: `hook`, `word`, `myth-fact`, `alert`, `cta`; `karaoke` captions; punch-ins at
  1.15–1.2; `focus` in a tall frame; `neon`, `signal` or `bloom` preset; `cut` transitions.
  See `shorts.md`.

## The walkthrough (screencasts, tutorials)

- The screen is the picture; the head is a small, constant presence.
- Callouts point at the thing on the screen the speaker is talking about, at the moment they
  talk about it. Keyboard shortcuts are shown as keys.
- Chapters are steps: "1 · Install", "2 · Configure".
- Fabula: `pip` with `screen` graphics wherever `get_framing` reports a screen span; `keys`,
  `code`, `alert`; `section` with numbers; `terminal` or `slate` preset.

## Borrowing well

Borrow the method, not the look: how a style decides *when* to show something, *how long*
to hold it, and *how much* to say on screen. The look is the theme, and the theme is the
person's — ask what the film is for and whether they have a saved brand before choosing one.
